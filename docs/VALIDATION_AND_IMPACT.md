# PinjamLab: SDG evidence and pilot plan

**Project:** PinjamLab: Circular Equipment Sharing for Inclusive Learning  
**Team:** KEBAS (Keluarga Bebas), Universitas Mikroskil, Indonesia

## Evidence status

The submission contains working software and an illustrative workspace. Technical checks can show that particular actions and constraints behave as expected in the checked environment. They do not establish that students have gained learning opportunities or that fewer tools have been bought or discarded. No real user pilot has been completed, and no measured environmental or financial saving is claimed.

## Relationship to the selected goals

| SDG target | Mechanism in the application | Evidence available in this submission | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.3: access to quality technical, vocational and tertiary education | A searchable catalogue, date requests, transparent request status and an explainable access-review queue help arrange access to learning tools. | Demonstrated workflow; persistent request and loan records; checks of permissions and allocation behavior. | Observe whether users can complete requests, obtain suitable equipment and participate in practical learning. Study which learners still face barriers. |
| 12.2: efficient use of natural resources | Share an existing stock and inspect recorded availability rather than treating every need as a new purchase. | Asset quantities, dated reservations and database-derived activity summaries. | A defined observation period with asset availability and actual use denominators; procurement records if making a purchasing claim. |
| 12.5: waste prevention, reduction and reuse | Record return condition, block affected equipment for care, record a resolution and make later use traceable. | Condition and maintenance workflow; records associated with an asset. | Verified repair and subsequent use; disposal records and a defensible comparison before claiming waste avoided. |

The mapping describes contribution pathways. It does not claim that this local application measures the UN's national SDG indicators. A completed loan is not a unit of carbon reduction, and a maintenance record is not proof that an item would otherwise have been discarded.

## Proposed supervised pilot

The following is a plan, not completed research. Begin only after the laboratory and institution agree to the scope and appropriate handling of participant data.

**Proposed participants:** 6-10 students and 1-2 laboratory staff. Select students with varied familiarity with the borrowing process and include relevant accessibility needs without collecting unnecessary sensitive data. The sample would support formative learning rather than population-wide conclusions.

**Proposed duration:** one supervised task session per participant, followed by an agreed short operational observation period if the laboratory authorizes real borrowing. Record the actual dates and duration when conducted.

### Preparation and consent

1. Obtain the laboratory's permission and define who can approve real handovers. Keep technical demonstration accounts separate from live participants.
2. Explain the task, voluntary participation, recorded information and retention period. Record consent in the institution's accepted form. Do not publish names, student IDs or contact details in the study data.
3. Prepare equivalent task pairs for the current process and PinjamLab. Alternate the order across participants to reduce practice effects. Define the start and end of each task before testing.
4. Verify inventory and inspection state with the laboratory before any real allocation. Staff retains authority over equipment suitability and safe use.

### Tasks

- Find a tool suitable for a specified practical-learning task and explain its availability for the requested dates.
- Submit a valid request and explain what pending status means.
- As staff, review two requests, explain their order and try an allocation that would exceed capacity.
- Record a handover and return. Flag a condition issue, confirm the affected quantity is held, then record an authorized resolution.
- Find the relevant record later and interpret an insight or export without treating illustrative values as field results.

Use a sandbox for conflict, damage and maintenance scenarios. Do not invent real damage or falsely record a real inspection just to complete a test task.

## Measurement definitions

| Measure | Operational definition | Reporting condition |
|---|---|---|
| Request completion time | Elapsed time from task start to a valid submitted request. Report assistance and failures separately. | Use equivalent tasks and consistent timing. Report actual observations, median/range and sample size. |
| Successful access | Eligible requests that lead to a confirmed, suitable handover within the agreed period divided by eligible requests observed. | State eligibility and missing-data rules. Approval alone is not a completed handover. |
| Approval conflict | A confirmed allocation that exceeds capacity for any relevant date or overlaps an unavailable unit. | Separate prevented attempts from conflicts that actually occurred. |
| Return timeliness | Completed loans returned on or before their recorded due date divided by completed loans with a verifiable return date. | State the date convention and exclude or explain missing return records. |
| Recorded equipment use | Completed loans, distinct borrowers and periods of recorded use. | True utilization requires a defined available-time denominator; do not rename a loan count utilization. |
| Use after care | An asset with a documented maintenance resolution and a later authorized completed loan. | Verify the records refer to the same asset and time order. This does not quantify avoided waste. |
| Clarity | A short post-task rating plus the participant's explanation of availability, status and queue reasons. | Quote only with consent and report negative feedback as well as positive feedback. |

## Observation record

Use pseudonymous participant codes. Suggested fields are task ID, process used, task order, start/end time, completion status, assistance, error type, request outcome, staff exception and the participant's explanation. Maintain any link between codes and identities in a separate restricted record, only if necessary. Never include KTM images, phone numbers, real account credentials or private participant email addresses in a public repository.

Before publishing, verify the notes with the responsible staff, remove identifiable details, and record the number of missing or excluded observations. Follow the agreed retention schedule. Do not create plausible-looking completed rows before collecting observations.

## Evaluate the access policy

Fewer completed loans in the last 30 days is an explainable operational rule. It does not measure income, need or disadvantage, and it can miss access obtained outside this system. Inspect whether repeat coursework requirements are unfairly affected, whether newcomers obtain suitable tools, and when staff overrides are justified. Report exceptions and improve the policy with the institution rather than assuming the queue proves equity.

## Reporting the actual pilot

When the pilot is performed, replace plans with dated methods and actual observations. Give participant numbers, task failures, assistance, staff exceptions, missing data and limitations. Keep technical test results separate from user-study outcomes. Environmental or financial estimates need a defined method, source records, assumptions and uncertainty; omit them until those inputs exist.
